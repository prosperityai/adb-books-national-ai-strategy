## 1. Design principles

- Train by role, not by enthusiasm. Each tier matches what people
  actually decide or do.
- Use local examples, local languages and the government's own tools.
- Assess people at the end. Attendance is not competence.
- Refresh yearly: tools and rules change.

## 3. Tier 1: Everyone (4 hours)

Learning outcomes. After this tier a participant can:

1. Explain in plain words what an AI system does and does not do.
2. Name three ways AI output can be wrong (errors, bias, made-up facts).
3. State the government's rules on which data may be entered into AI
   tools, and where those rules are published.
4. Report a suspected AI-related incident.

Modules:

| Module | Content | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | What AI is: prediction, generation, classification | 45 min |
| 1.2 | Where government already uses AI (local examples) | 45 min |
| 1.3 | Data rules: what never goes into a public AI tool | 60 min |
| 1.4 | Errors, bias and checking outputs | 60 min |
| 1.5 | Quiz (pass mark ____%) | 30 min |

## 4. Tier 2: Users (8 hours)

Outcomes: use the approved assistant for drafting, summarising and
translation; check outputs against sources; record AI use in files.

| Module | Content | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | The approved assistant: access, logging, limits | 1 h |
| 2.2 | Drafting letters, briefs and minutes with checks | 2 h |
| 2.3 | Summarising and translating in ____________ (languages) | 2 h |
| 2.4 | When not to use AI (decisions about individuals) | 1 h |
| 2.5 | Workshop: improve a real document from your desk | 1.5 h |
| 2.6 | Practical assessment | 0.5 h |

## 5. Tier 3: Buyers and managers (24 hours)

Outcomes: write an outcome-based specification; evaluate bids on data
rights, portability and exit; run a pilot with a stage gate.

| Day | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Use-case scoring (portfolio tool); writing the problem statement; cost drivers (tokens, GPUs, staff) |
| 2 | Procurement clauses (data rights, model portability, audit, exit); evaluation scoring; vendor demos and how to test them |
| 3 | Pilot design, success measures, stage gates; contract management; case exercise and assessment |

## 6. Tier 4: Policy and oversight (32 hours)

Outcomes: run an algorithmic impact assessment; apply the data
protection law to an AI case; question a supplier's evaluation results.

| Day | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | How models are built and evaluated; reading a model card |
| 2 | Data protection law applied to AI; lawful bases; sharing agreements |
| 3 | Algorithmic impact assessment workshop on a real system |
| 4 | Audit, redress and incident handling; assessed case presentation |

## 7. Tier 5: Builders (120+ hours)

Outcomes: deploy and monitor a model on government infrastructure;
build evaluation sets in local languages; operate securely.

| Block | Content | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Python and data handling refresher | 16 |
| 5.2 | Serving open models; measuring throughput and cost | 24 |
| 5.3 | Retrieval over government documents | 24 |
| 5.4 | Evaluation: test sets, group metrics, red-teaming | 24 |
| 5.5 | Security, logging and incident response | 16 |
| 5.6 | Capstone project for a real ministry problem | 16+ |

Completion requires a working capstone reviewed by a senior engineer.

## 8. Leaders briefing (3 hours)

| Segment | Content | Time |
|---|---|---|
| L.1 | What the strategy commits the government to, and the budget | 45 min |
| L.2 | Three live systems: what worked, what failed, what it cost | 60 min |
| L.3 | Risks a minister must ask about (questions card) | 45 min |
| L.4 | Decisions needed from this group | 30 min |
